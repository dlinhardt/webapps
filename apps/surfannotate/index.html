Cortical surface annotation in the browser

Delineate areas on a cortical surface, and export them.

Load a FreeSurfer or GIfTI surface — folded, inflated, spherical, or an unfolded flat — and mark regions by clicking along their border. The points are joined by the shortest path across the mesh, so the border follows the surface rather than a straight line through it. Areas are drawn in sequence and partition the surface: each one's border becomes the edge the next is closed against, so neighbouring areas share a boundary exactly instead of having overlapping vertices.

Your surfaces, overlays and labels never leave this machine — there is no server to send them to.

How it works

1

Load a surface

Pick a file or drop it on the viewer. Load several and switch between them; an area drawn on the inflated surface is still there on the folded one.

2

Click the border

Nothing is traced while you click, hold and hover around the surface, so you can rotate freely. Close the border and fill it. On a cut surface the edge of the flat surface closes it for you — two points can be enough.

3

Save and export

Saved areas become the edges the next ones are drawn against, and can be reopened to adjust. Export each as a .label or .label.gii.

Everything runs in this browser tab — no upload, no account, no server. MIT licensed · rendering by NiiVue

        Areas

          Areas partition the surface: each is drawn on what the areas above it have left, so no vertex belongs to two. Its border then works like the edge of a flat surface — the next area can be closed against it, on folded surfaces too.
          Editing one re-derives the areas below it, so moving a shared boundary moves both sides.
          ▲▼ reorder — later areas give way to earlier ones. ✎ reopens an area to adjust its border. Click a name to export it instead of the region being drawn.
